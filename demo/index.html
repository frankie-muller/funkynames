funkynames

Readable handles and higher-entropy codes from six curated pools — and, unusually, the arithmetic that says what they're worth. A three-word name feels about as unguessable as a password. It isn't, and the gap is where the bugs live. Source on GitHub.

Pick a handle

A handle is three words in a fixed order — a descriptor, then a colour or action, then a place or thing — drawn from six pools that were curated for how they sound together. The grammar is what makes it read like a name; no word ever repeats inside one handle. Seven are generated below, in your browser, by the same library the package ships. No name you generate leaves this page — the only thing it fetches is the typeface.

Read them aloud. Star the ones that stick, then reroll the rest — starred handles stay put, everything else is replaced. After two or three rerolls, the one you can still say without looking is the memorable one. Click it to choose it: you'll see which pool each word came from, and how many handles were possible at this length range.

to Applies to every panel on the page. Changing it deals a fresh seven; starred handles that still fit are kept.
Keyboard: Tab between options, Enter on a name to choose it, Enter on ☆ or S to star

Roll some codes

Codes are the other shape. Where a handle keeps a grammar, a code draws every word from all six pools merged, in any order, with repeats allowed — so it reads less like a name and more like a passphrase, and each word is worth more bits. Use handles for things people will say and remember; use codes for invite links, room codes and anything a stranger might try to guess. The slider sets how many words; the "Take it apart" panel below shows what each extra word buys you.

    Take it apart

    Turn pools off, pick how many words to analyse, then set the attack you're worried about. (Word length is set once, at the top of the page, and applies here too.) The last slider is the one people forget: an attacker doesn't want your name, they want any live one — so a big keyspace gets diluted by a big population.

    Is this safe for…

    Judges the Take it apart settings — its pools, its word count, its word length — against what each use actually demands. The codes above are just examples; this panel measures the shape, not a specific code.
